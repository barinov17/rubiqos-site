Proforma.

Every product concept of the island as a card: what it is, how many people it holds, what it is made of, who runs it and what it needs every day — water, energy, sewage, food, laundry, waste. Below them the services that supply those needs, and the balance between demand and capacity. Numbers are planning ratios for a luxury island resort on master plan v024; replace them with the operator's own as they arrive.

Product concepts

Revenue-generating units. Capacity is the most people the concept can hold; the needs are for an average day at 70 % occupancy.

Services

Back-of-house capacity that the concepts draw on. Green figures are capacities.

RubiqOS · proforma of the generic island resort, 17 October 2026. Method: needs = capacity × occupancy (× visits a day for day venues) × a ratio per guest-day or per visit; the marina is counted per occupied berth. Ratios per guest-day: hotels water 500 L, energy 42 kWh, food 2.0 kg, laundry 4 kg, waste 2.5 kg; villas 600 L, 50 kWh, 1.5 kg, 4 kg, 2.5 kg; residences 300 L, 25 kWh, 1.0 kg, 1.5 kg, 2.0 kg. Day venues per visit: 15–180 L of water, 4–12 kWh, 0.1–0.9 kg of food, 0.1–2 kg of laundry, 0.2–0.6 kg of waste. Berth-day: 15 m boat 0.2 m³ and 60 kWh, 35 m boat 1 m³ and 400 kWh, 80 m boat 5 m³ and 2,400 kWh of shore power. Sewage = 85 % of water. Sources: Cornell Hotel Sustainability Benchmarking (luxury resort tier), ITP water and carbon benchmarks, UNEP tourism waste ratios, PIANC marina guidance. Guests on the average day = capacity × 70 %; hotel practice applies occupancy to keys at about two guests per key, which gives some 15 % fewer guests, so these figures sit on the safe side. Working material, not an offer.
